الأفعال العربية

Arabic Verbs: A Study Guide by Level

Essential grammar for non-native speakers.

Novice Level: The Basics of the Root

الجذر الثلاثي (The Three-Letter Root)

All Arabic verbs are built on a root, usually three letters (e.g., K-T-B). This root carries the core meaning.

ك-ت-ب (K-T-B) — Core meaning related to Writing.

الأزمنة الثلاثة (The Three Tenses)

  • الماضي (Al-Māḍī - Past Tense): What happened. Formed by adding suffixes to the root. جَلَسَ (Jalasa) - He sat.
  • المضارع (Al-Muḍāri’ - Present Tense): What is happening/will happen. Formed by adding prefixes and suffixes. يَجْلِسُ (Yajlisu) - He sits/is sitting.
  • الأمر (Al-Amr - Command Tense): An instruction. اِجْلِسْ (Ijliss) - Sit! (m. singular)

الوزن الأول (Form I - The Base Form)

Most common verbs fall into Form I. You must memorize the middle vowel sound (e.g., *kataba* or *daraba*).

Root Past Tense (هو) Meaning
ف-ع-لفَعَلَTo do
د-ر-سدَرَسَTo study
أ-ك-لأَكَلَTo eat

Intermediate Level: The Derived Forms (الأوزان)

المزيد (Derived Forms II, III, IV)

Forms II, III, and IV are the most common derived forms. They often change the meaning dramatically (causative, intensive, or participatory).

Form Pattern (هو) Example (علم) Meaning
IIفَعَّلَعَلَّمَTo teach (intensive)
IIIفَاعَلَعَالَجَTo treat (participatory)
IVأَفْعَلَأَعْلَنَTo announce (causative)

الأفعال المعتلّة (Weak Verbs)

Verbs containing one of the weak letters (ا, و, ي - *alif, wāw, yā’*). Their conjugation is irregular.

  • Assimilated (مثال): Weak letter is the 1st root letter (e.g., وَجَدَ - *wajada* - To find). The *wāw* often disappears in the present tense (يَجِدُ - *yajidu*).
  • Hollow (أجوف): Weak letter is the 2nd root letter (e.g., قَالَ - *qāla* - To say). The weak letter often changes or drops in conjugation (قُلْتُ - *qultu* - I said).
  • Defective (ناقص): Weak letter is the 3rd root letter (e.g., رَمَى - *ramā* - To throw). The weak letter changes to *alif* or *yā’* depending on the conjugation.

الاسم الفاعل والمفعول (Active and Passive Participles)

These are verb-based nouns and adjectives. For derived forms (II-X), they follow the pattern: *mu-* + root + vowel change.

عَلَّمَ (Form II: Taught) → مُعَلِّم (Mu'allim - Teacher/Active Participle)

كَتَبَ (Form I: Wrote) → كَاتِب (Kātib - Writer/Active Participle)

Advanced Level: Full Paradigm & Advanced Irregulars

بقية الأوزان (Remaining Forms V-X)

These forms are often reflexive, passive, or indicate seeking/requesting.

Form Pattern (هو) Meaning Hint
VتَفَعَّلَReflexive of II (e.g., to learn)
VIتَفَاعَلَReciprocal of III (e.g., to correspond)
VIIاِنْفَعَلَPassive/Reflexive (e.g., to break)
VIIIاِفْتَعَلَActive/Reflexive (e.g., to gather)
XاِسْتَفْعَلَSeeking/Requesting (e.g., to use/seek use)

المضعف (Geminate/Doubled Verbs)

Verbs where the 2nd and 3rd root letters are identical (e.g., M-D-D).

مَدَّ (Madda - He extended). In the past tense, the geminate un-doubles when followed by a consonant suffix:

مَدَدْتُ (Madadtu - I extended).

الأفعال الرباعية (Quadriliteral Verbs)

Verbs based on a four-letter root (Form I is the only one, with a derived Form II).

Root: د-ح-ر-ج → Form I: دَحْرَجَ (Daḥraja - To roll/trundle)

Derived Form: تَدَحْرَجَ (Tadaḥraja - To roll *itself* - reflexive)
